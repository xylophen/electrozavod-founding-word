# Research: Guaraní for the Founding Word of Electrozavod

## English summary of findings

**Variety.** Use **Paraguayan Guaraní** (*avañe'ẽ* / *guarani paraguayo*), the official language of the Republic alongside Spanish. Do not use Brazilian Nheengatu, Bolivian Ava-Guaraní, Mbya, or other family varieties unless a later specialist review argues otherwise. Formal public writing in Paraguay is this variety.

**Orthography.** Use the **Academia de la Lengua Guaraní (ALG / Guarani Ñe'ẽ Rerekuapavẽ) 2016 four-rule standard**: 33 native graphemes including nasal vowels, *g̃*, digraphs (*ch, mb, nd, ng, nt, rr*), and the *puso* ('); *D, F, LL* only for regulated loans. This is the legal successor to the 1992 Constitution alphabet and to earlier MEC school conventions (1994/2004). MEC and SPL are now aligning educational materials to the Academy, not the other way around.

**Register.** For a founding covenant, write **solemn civic Guaraní** — the same family as the 1992 Constitution (*Ñande Léi Guasu*), the official bilingual *Ley 4251/10 De Lenguas*, and SPL administrative covenants — with a biblical-adjacent cadence drawn from *Ñandejára Ñe'ẽ* (Sociedad Bíblica Paraguaya), where "covenant/pact" is *ñe'ẽ ñome'ẽ*. This is **not** street *jopará*. It is also **not** Jesuit-mission "pure" reconstruction. Official civic Guaraní already admits regulated Spanish loans (*derécho, léi, presupuesto, compañía, público*) while keeping Guaraní syntax, postpositions, and moral vocabulary (*teko marangatu, tesakã, tekojoja, ñe'ẽme'ẽ*).

**Key recommendation — publishing as final: NO-GO without human review.** A researched draft can be produced and labeled as such. Publishing that draft as the company's sacred-feeling founding text, without a native speaker who writes formal Guaraní (ideally an ALG / Ateneo / UNA / SPL translator), would be irresponsible. Machine and AI Guaraní routinely invent words, break nasal harmony, calque Spanish word order, and miss the exclusive/inclusive *we*. Paraguayans who actually speak the language will hear those errors immediately. Label any AI draft: **researched working draft, pending specialist review — not final.**

## 1. Variety and orthography for formal Paraguayan public writing

### 1.1 Which Guaraní?

Paraguay's official language pair is Spanish and **Guaraní** (Constitution art. 140; Ley 4251/10 arts. 3, 14). The Academy's legal mandate is to normativize "**la lengua guaraní hablada en el Paraguay**" and to include historical vocabulary **and** "los que se hallan en uso en la sociedad paraguaya" (Ley 4251/10 art. 45–46; SPL institutional page).

That is **Guaraní paraguayo / avañe'ẽ**, not:

- Mbya, Ava Guaraní, Pai Tavyterã, or other indigenous-community varieties (those are protected separately under the same law);
- Argentine Correntino Guaraní (related, not the Paraguayan public standard);
- Brazilian Nheengatu or other Tupi-Guaraní languages.

For a Naviera whose audience is Paraguayans, the public standard is the only defensible choice.

Miguel Ángel Verón (ALG académico de número) prefers the label **"guaraní paraguayo"**: Guaraní morphosyntax plus integrated Hispanicisms as loans — as distinct from unregulated code-mixing he still hears called *jopará* (Rodríguez 2025 interview). That is the target: **Guaraní structure, disciplined loans, not a Spanish paragraph with Guaraní nouns sprinkled in.**

### 1.2 Official orthography: ALG 2016, not a private "ACA" house style

There is no competing private "ACA" spelling that outranks the Academy. The legal chain is:

1. **Constitución Nacional 1992** — first official state text in Guaraní; its alphabet is the statutory base (Ley 4251/10 art. 46.2: "Establecer el alfabeto guaraní sobre la base del utilizado en la Convención Nacional Constituyente de 1992").
2. **Ley 4251/10 De Lenguas** — creates SPL and the Academia; gives the Academy competence over orthography, lexicon, terminology, grammar, and discourse (arts. 43–46). Written implementation of bilingual public texts became demandable three years after the Academy set the alphabet and official grammar (art. 51).
3. **ALG alphabet** — approved 4 November 2015 (33 phonemes/graphemes); four orthographic rules approved 10 August 2016 and presented 7 September 2016; later printed in the *Gaceta Oficial* (2017) and restated in *Guarani Ñe'ẽtekuaa* (ALG grammar, 2018).
4. **MEC 1994 / 2004 school conventions** — earlier educational alphabet, already 33 graphemes including *g̃* and *puso*, plus *d, f, ll* for loans. Historically important; **not** the current legal standard. SPL–MEC–ALG are now writing a joint norm for school materials *to apply the Academy's standard*, not to revive MEC 2004 as a rival (SPL news, interinstitutional mesa).

**The four ALG rules (2016 / Gaceta Oficial 2017):**

| Rule | Content |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Official alphabet | 33 native graphemes: *a ã ch e ẽ g g̃ h i ĩ j k l m mb n nd ng nt ñ o õ p r rr s t u ũ v y ỹ '* (*puso*). *D, F, LL* only for regulated foreign words. No silent letters; digraphs count as one letter. |
| 2. Accent mark | One acute accent per word, only when the stressed vowel is **not** word-final (e.g. *ára, purahéi, mbo'ehára*). Nasal vowels do **not** take an additional acute (*mokõi, ko'ẽrõ*). |
| 3. Nasal syllables | Nasal harmony / nasal syllable formation (tilde ~ on the relevant vowel; *g̃*, *ỹ*, etc.). |
| 4. Word formation | Prefixes, suffixes, and **monosyllabic** postpositions attach to the host; **polysyllabic** postpositions are written separate (*ou rehe*, *amo guive*). This is the rule that most AI drafts get wrong. |

Practical typing notes for our files:

- Prefer precomposed *ã ẽ ĩ õ ũ ỹ Ã Ẽ Ĩ Õ Ũ Ỹ* and *g̃ / G̃* (U+0067 + U+0303). Avoid the old circumflex-for-nasal Jesuit/popular spellings (*â ê î*) in a new public text.
- Use the straight apostrophe *'* for *puso* (ALG name: *puso*).
- Keep proper names that are not Guaraní in their source form: **Electrozavod**, **Naviera** (as a company-type title). **Paraguay** in running Guaraní is **Paraguái** (Ley 4251 bilingual edition; Constitution Guaraní).

### 1.3 Recommendation

Write the Founding Word in **ALG 2016 orthography**, in **Guaraní paraguayo**, modeled on **SPL / Ley 4251 bilingual civic prose**. Do not invent a more "indigenous" spelling to look older, and do not drop *g̃* or *puso* to look modern.

## 2. Register: what a founding covenant should sound like

### 2.1 The problem of tone

The English master is elevated, covenantal, biblical-adjacent civic language: "Word," "covenant," "charge," "the dead and the living," "no sacred persons." A Paraguayan audience will hear this in two traditions at once:

- **Civic-constitutional** — *Léi Guasu*, laws, municipal bilingual notices, SPL covenants.
- **Biblical-ecclesial** — *Ñandejára Ñe'ẽ*, Catholic and Protestant preaching, the everyday solemnity of *ñe'ẽme'ẽ* (a given word / vow).

Both traditions are already in Guaraní. Neither is street *jopará*. Neither is a word-for-word Jesuit reconstruction. The right register is **solemn civic Guaraní with a biblical cadence**, not a pastiche of Spanish legalisms and not an ethnographic museum piece.

### 2.3 What "solemn" does *not* mean

- It does not mean **maximum purism**. Official civic Guaraní already says *derécho, léi, presupuesto*. A founding charter that refuses every Spanish legal word will sound like a school exam, not like the *Léi Guasu*.
- It does not mean **jopará as the base**. *Jopará* is the vital oral continuum (Kallfell 2016; Penner 2016; Rodríguez 2025). It is the wrong written register for a covenant meant to be read in assembly and handed down. Verón's line is the right one: jopará is what is spoken; it is almost barred from formal materials — and a founding Word is a formal material.
- It does not mean **translating English metaphors one-for-one**. "Open vein of the Southern Cone," "civilizational fire," "the human project" will die if calqued. They need Guaraní images that a river people already have (*ysyry, rape, tatapỹ, teko*).

### 2.4 Voice: *ore* vs *ñande*

Guaraní distinguishes **exclusive *ore*** (we, not you) from **inclusive *ñande*** (we, including you). The English "we set down this Word" is a founding *we* speaking **to** those who come after. Official recommendation:

- **ore** for the founders' acts (we set this down; we take up this work);
- **ñande** when the text speaks of the land, the people, or a shared inheritance (*ñane retã, ñande ysyry*);
- third person for the company as an institution (*Electrozavod oñemopu'ã …*).

This is one of the first things a native reviewer must check. AI drafts almost always collapse it.

## 3. Known pitfalls

### 3.1 Jopará vs "purer" Guaraní

The live debate in Paraguay is not "use Guaraní or don't." It is **which Guaraní counts as written**.

- **Jopará / jehe'a**: everyday mixed repertoire; Guaraní grammar with a high and variable Spanish lexicon; mainly oral (Kallfell 2016; Canese Caballero 2009 cited in Virginia Tech thesis). Dietrich has even called *guaraniete* a "virtual purity" that exists chiefly in writing.
- **Guaraní académico / guaraniete / guaraní normativo**: the school and Academy target. Teachers and rural speakers often experience it as foreign; neologisms for everyday things (*jahuha* vs *baño*) fail outside the classroom (Mortimer 2006/2016; Penner 2016).
- **Guaraní paraguayo** (Verón): Guaraní morphosyntax + **integrated** Hispanicisms, as opposed to free code-switching.

For this covenant: **write Guaraní paraguayo in the civic-solemn register.** Keep Spanish loans that official texts already keep. Do not write *el río que lleva la fuerza del continente* with Guaraní nouns dropped in. Do not replace *Naviera* with an invented 12-morpheme neologism that no sailor would say.

### 3.2 When Spanish loans are acceptable in formal Guaraní

Evidence from texts the State has already published:

**Keep as loans (ALG allows *d, f, ll* once regulated; civic practice already does this):**

- Proper names and titles: *Electrozavod, Naviera, Occidente* (or *Kuarahy reike*, see glossary).
- Legal-administrative nouns already in Ley 4251 / CN: *léi, derécho, presupuesto, título, público, compañía / empresa, capital* (when the financial sense is intended and *viru / mba'erepy* would narrow it).
- International or technical nouns with no stable civic equivalent yet: *Occidente* as a civilizational name; *Naviera* as a sector title Paraguayans already use on the river.

**Prefer Guaraní when the official or biblical corpus already has a living word:**

- covenant → *ñe'ẽ ñome'ẽ / ñe'ẽme'ẽ*
- dignity → *teko marangatu*
- justice → *tekojoja*
- government → *tekuái*
- property → *mba'etee*
- people / citizens → *tetãygua*
- river → *ysyry*
- word / founding word → *ñe'ẽ / ñe'ẽ moñepyrũmby*
- money → *viru*
- clarity / transparency → *tesakã*

**Avoid:**

- inventing Guaraní "equivalents" that only exist in one textbook (*jahuha*-style);
- leaving a Spanish verb conjugated inside a Guaraní clause (*ore decidimos* …);
- Spanish SVO calques where Guaraní wants a postposition or a verb-final clause;
- attaching or detaching postpositions against ALG rule 4.

### 3.3 Hard governance terms

These are the words that will make or break the text. Full proposed renderings are in §6. The principle:

1. Look first in **CN 1992 Guaraní** and **Ley 4251 bilingual**.
2. Then in **ALG dictionary / grammar** and **Ateneo / Galeano** civic lists.
3. Then in **biblical Guaraní** for moral-covenantal senses.
4. If still unstable, **keep the Spanish loan** and put a Guaraní gloss once, rather than minting a private neologism in a founding charter.

### 3.4 AI / machine-translation failure modes (why this is not "done")

Documented and observed limits, not vibes:

- Invented or textbook-only neologisms that native speakers reject (Mortimer; Penner; La Nación 2025 interview on academic Guaraní vs living speech).
- Collapse of *ore / ñande*.
- Broken nasal harmony and missing *puso / g̃*.
- Spanish word order under a Guaraní lexicon (calque).
- Wrong postposition attachment (ALG rule 4).
- Loss of solemn cadence — the English is biblical-civic; a literal AI pass becomes a school paragraph.

An academic of the ALG, speaking in 2025 about putting the language into AI, warned that doing so "así como está nuestro guaraní" would be *imprudente* without an epistemological reset that starts from the speaker (La Nación, 31 Aug 2025). That warning applies directly to publishing this charter from a model output.

## 5. Human review: strongly recommended, and how to label the draft

**Recommendation: do not call any Guaraní version "final" until a human who writes formal Paraguayan Guaraní has revised it.**

This is not politeness. It is the same standard Paraguay applies to its own laws: Spanish is the promulgated text; the Guaraní is a solemn official version that still required an Academy, a grammar, and years of institutional work (Ley 4251/10 arts. 14, 51). A company founding covenant that claims to speak "the language of the land" so that "none may say it was hidden" cannot be the one text we treat more casually than a municipal ordinance.

**What "human review" means here**

Minimum (one reviewer):

- a native or near-native speaker of **Guaraní paraguayo** who also **writes** it (school / university / SPL / church / journalism);
- briefed on ALG 2016 orthography and on the English/Spanish masters;
- asked to fix voice (*ore/ñande*), loans, cadence, and any invented words — not just to "approve the vibe."

Strong (what the text deserves):

- one **literary / civic translator** (Ateneo, UNA ISL, or an ALG member);
- one **river / Asunción reader** who is not a grammarian, to catch academic frost;
- a short bilingual back-translation into Spanish to confirm nothing sacred was inverted.

Institutions a founder can actually write to:

- Secretaría de Políticas Lingüísticas — Dirección General de Planificación Lingüística (they already produce official bilingual instruments).
- Academia de la Lengua Guaraní — Departamento de Literatura y Publicaciones / Lexicografía.
- Ateneo de Lengua y Cultura Guaraní.
- Instituto Superior de Lenguas, UNA.
- A named independent: e.g. work associated with Miguel Ángel Verón, David Galeano Olivera, or other públicos listed on the SPL Academy page — approached as paid professionals, not as unpaid validators.

**Labeling rule for our file `06-guarani-draft.md`:**

> Researched working draft in ALG 2016 orthography. Not final. Not for engraving, notarial deposit, or public proclamation as the Guaraní Word until a specialist has signed off. If Spanish and Guaraní ever conflict, the English archival master (and its Spanish working translation) still govern intent until the Guaraní is revised.

| debt (moral, historical) | **jehepy / mba'e ojehepy'ỹva** | medium | Do not use *deuda* as the only word. The Triple Alliance grief should stay dignified: **Ñorairõ Guasu** is the name Paraguayans already use for that slaughter — a reviewer should decide whether to name it or keep the English's unnamed "great slaughter." |
| West / civilizational inheritance | **Occidente** (loan) + *kuarahy reike gotyo arandupy* | low–medium | A private neologism for "the West" would be the worst kind of academic frost. Keep *Occidente* as the civilizational name; explain once if needed. |
| excellence | **katupyry / tembiapo porã** | medium | *Ikatupyry* is competence. "Excellence is not optional" → *Katupyry niko natekotevẽi ojeiporavo; tekotevẽ.* |
| resilience | **teko py'aguasu / jejopykue** | low | No stable civic equivalent found. Periphrase: *ñañemoĩva'erã ñande rakãkuépe, yvóra natekotevẽi ojekuaa rupi.* Or keep *resiliencia* once. |
| mediocrity | **mba'e reíre / tembiapo vai kangy** | low | Periphrase, do not mint. |
| assembly | **amandaje / aty** | high | *Amandaje Guasu* = Congress (Ley 4251). For a company assembly, *aty* or *amandaje*. |
| founder | **moñepyrũhára** | medium | Well-formed. |
| charge / mandate | **tembiaporã** | high | Ley 4251 uses *tembiaporã* throughout for duty/charge. |
| common wealth / common hope | **mba'erepy oñondivegua / ñeha'arõ oñondivegua** | medium | |
| Southern Cone | **Cono Sur** (loan) | medium | Keep; a coined Guaraní geography would look invented. |
| open vein | **tuguai / tuguái ijurúva** or **ysyry rape ijurúva** | low | The English metaphor is Galeano-inflected Spanish-American. A reviewer should choose an image that does not sound like a bad tattoo. Draft uses *ysyry rape ijurúva* (the river road that lies open). |

## 7. Translation strategy (how the draft was built)

1. **Pivot from the English master**, with the Spanish Cono Sur version as a sense-check (Paraguayans will often meet the charter in Spanish first). Do not translate the Spanish; translate the English through a civic-Guaraní lens.
2. **Model sentences on Ley 4251 bilingual** for institutional voice and on **Hebrews 8 GDC** for covenantal cadence.
3. **Unpack** English abstracts (capital, resilience, civilizational fire, human project) into Guaraní clauses rather than one-word inventions.
4. **Name what Paraguay already names** (*Paraguái, Ysyry Paraguái, Ñorairõ Guasu* as a reviewer option, *Naviera, Electrozavod*).
5. **Keep the closing formula close**, because it is the sentence people will remember: *Ko'ápe, yvy ape ári ijurúva ysyry rape rupi, peteĩ tetãygua oñemandu'a ijeheguiete rehe ha omopu'ã hekópe.*
6. **Refuse to declare victory.** The draft in `06-guarani-draft.md` is a working text for a human to cut.

# The Founding Word of Electrozavod

**(A Covenant for the River and the People)**

In the days when the powers of the age grow strange and the measure of men is tested, we set down this Word.
Not as the decrees of merchants, nor as the fleeting laws of assemblies that pass with the season, but as a covenant spoken in the hearing of those who come after, that they may know why this work was begun and upon what foundation it must stand or fall.

## Of the Place and the Debt

There is a land where the great waters gather and flow southward, carrying the strength of the continent toward the sea. This land is called Paraguay, and the river that runs through its heart is the open vein of the Southern Cone.
Upon this soil a great slaughter was once wrought, and the blood of a people was poured out until the land itself seemed emptied. That debt cannot be repaid by gold or by speech. It remains.
Yet it is given to the living to answer the dead not with despair, but with fidelity. Therefore we take up this work in the shadow of that memory, that the river which once bore sorrow may in time bear life, and that the children of this land may stand upright again beneath the open sky.

## Of the Purpose

Electrozavod is raised to be the Naviera of the Paraguayans — not in name only, but in truth.
Its first charge is this: that the movement of goods upon the waters shall serve the increase of the people’s strength, their knowledge, and their freedom. Profit is the servant, never the master. Where the two contend, the good of the people shall prevail.
We hold that every person bears an inherent dignity that no power may justly erase. We hold that rules must be written, equal, and binding upon the high as upon the low. We hold that truth is to be sought without fear, and that excellence in the work of the hands and of the mind is a duty owed to the living and to those yet unborn.
These things we receive from the long inheritance of the West — from the law that binds without respect of persons, from the love of wisdom that questions even the mighty, and from the recognition that each soul is of worth beyond measure. We do not impose a faith; we keep the civilizational fire that made steadfast cooperation and human flourishing possible across generations.

## Of the Binding Principles

These are the non-negotiable foundations. Let none who come after claim ignorance of them.

1. The company exists for the enduring good of the Paraguayan people and of those who share the life of the river. All other aims are subordinate.
2. Transparency shall be the default. What is done with the common wealth, the common ownership, and the common hope must be open to the light, save only where clear and temporary necessity of safety or fair dealing requires otherwise.
3. Corruption shall be made difficult by the very form of the work. Critical acts of allocation, ownership, and command shall be ordered so that no single hand may seize what belongs to the many without the knowledge of the many.
4. Every form of capital that enters this house — whether of money, of vessels, of skill, of trust, or of knowledge — must in time be turned toward shared fruit and toward the strengthening of the people. Nothing may remain as mere ornament of power.
5. We prepare for an age in which the labor of the mind becomes abundant. In that age the company must still serve, still teach, and still guard the dignity of those who live by the river.
6. Knowledge that can be shared without harm shall be shared. The tools of governance, of learning, and of the work itself shall lean toward openness, that many hands may improve what one mind begins.
7. No single path, single ally, or single strength shall be trusted alone. Resilience is required, for the world is uncertain and the proud are often brought low.
8. Power within this house is legitimate only while it serves. It must remain constrained, visible, and removable. The mission endures beyond any person who holds office within it.
9. Excellence is not optional. Shoddy work, hidden failure, and the quiet acceptance of mediocrity are betrayals of the covenant.
10. The rules bind the founders as tightly as they bind the least participant. There are no sacred persons.

## Of the River and the Hope

The river is older than our names. It will outlast our vessels and our words.
Yet for a time it is given into our keeping. If we move its burdens with honesty, if we turn a portion of its yield into the teaching of the young and the strengthening of the weak, if we refuse the old patterns of extraction and silence, then this place may become a refuge of competence and of hope when darker storms gather upon the earth.
Paraguay stands at a crossroads of waters and of histories. Should the greater world falter, it is not impossible that free and capable people here might yet preserve something of the human project — provided they have been formed in truth, in skill, and in the habit of upright dealing.
This is the charge.

## Closing

Let this Word be translated into the tongues of the West and into the language of the land, Guaraní, so that none may say it was hidden.
Let it be read in the assembly of those who take up the work, and again when they pass it to others.
If in later days the company forgets these things and becomes only another instrument of private gain or of quiet domination, then it will have broken faith with the dead and with the living, and its name will become a reproach.
But if it holds, even through difficulty, then those who come after may say:

> Here, upon the open vein of the continent, a people remembered their dignity and built accordingly.

This is the Founding Word of Electrozavod.

It is spoken in the present hour, for the days that are coming.
